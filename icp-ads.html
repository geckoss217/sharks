The rule

read first

These profiles exist to decide advertising content, keywords and themes. Nothing here is a sales process. That lives in the Sales Playbook.

Overlap rule. ICPs overlap by design. A daughter who owns her mother's house is both Profile 1 and Profile 8. Every target is advertised to under one primary ICP, and the secondary role is never used as a target. When two profiles compete for the same term, the term is assigned to one of them and only one.

Data source. All volume, competition and CPC figures below are live Google Keyword Planner pulls for the United States, taken August 22 and September 2 2026 from the Emergency Cleanings Google Ads account (customer 2998752313). Volumes are Google's rounded 12-month averages. Where several phrasings returned identical figures, Keyword Planner clustered them and the number is the cluster total, marked cluster.

Primary means the ad set leads with that service. Secondary means it appears on the landing page and never in the headline. A "No" means we do not advertise that service to that profile at all.

Hoarding is primary for four profiles and they must not share creative. Profile 1 is a family paying for someone else. Profile 5 is the person who lives there. Profile 6 is an agency acting on a housing order. Profile 8 discovers it mid-job. Same crew, four different ads.

Profile 9 is the only profile carrying two primaries. A single covered commercial loss can produce emergency cleanup, industrial cleanup, or both, and the carrier pays for whatever the adjuster scopes. Hoarding is marked No there because it is normally not a covered loss.

Where the volume actually is

assigned by profile

Two findings from the September pull change how Profiles 7 and 8 get targeted.

Landlords do not self-identify in search. Every landlord-qualified term returned no measurable volume: rental property cleanout, tenant cleanout, landlord junk removal, junk removal for landlords, abandoned property cleanout, distressed property cleanout, rental turnover cleanout, biohazard cleanup home. Profile 8 cannot be reached by an ICP-specific keyword. They search the generic cleanout terms, and we separate them with ad copy and landing pages instead.

Most commercial qualifiers are dead too. No volume for commercial cleanout, retail store cleanout, vacant building cleanout, commercial property cleanup, squatter cleanup, illegal dumping removal, commercial biohazard cleanup, vandalism cleanup, or tenant abandonment cleanup. Themes by profile

what the ad says

Each theme is a hypothesis to test, not a finished line. Every one of them has to survive a landing page that cannot quote a price, because no one in this category quotes before an on-site scope.

1The Family Caregiver

Hoarding. Three angles worth testing against each other: price certainty ("we look at the whole house, then we give you one price, and it does not move"), the parent's consent ("your mom gets to say what stays, nobody gets bulldozed"), and discretion ("nothing on our trucks says what we do"). The consent angle addresses the fear the research says is strongest and that no competitor speaks to directly.

Own the informational layer too. How to help a hoarder costs 98 cents a click and puts us in front of this person weeks before they search for a company.

2The Property Manager

Emergency. Test vacancy days ("call us the day you have access"), the hard units ("we take the units your regular guys will not"), and administrative relief ("credentialed, insured to your limits, photo report on every unit"). Do not reuse consumer hoarding language on this profile.

Search barely matters here. This profile is won on credentialing platforms and local apartment associations.

3The Estate Closer

Emergency, hoarding secondary. Test the date ("tell us your listing date, we will tell you if we can hit it"), the record ("a written record of everything that left the property"), and the agent angle ("we empty it, you list it"). The estate cluster is 7,700 a month at $10 to $12 and is Low competition.

4The Plant Manager

Industrial. Not a search profile. It is an outbound and prequalification motion timed to two annual peaks: refinery turnarounds peaking late February and March, and power shoulder seasons March to May and September to November. The conversation starts two to three quarters before the outage.

5The Resident

Hoarding, phased. Test the price barrier ("most people who call us cannot write one big check, so we built a way to pay a little at a time"), the standard ("we are not making it look like a magazine, we are making it safe"), and the first step ("start with one room"). FSSP signup is the call to action, never a phone number leading to a quote they cannot accept.

Small, contested, above $10 a click. Do not buy hoarding disorder treatment at 5,400 a month. That is a therapy search and it is the wrong intent at scale.

6The Agency Case Manager

Hoarding, emergency secondary. Not a paid-search profile. Reached through county vendor registration, the IOCDF task force directory, and LinkedIn. Test the file ("photos, a scope, and an invoice that fits your approval limit") and housing retention ("the goal is that your client keeps the apartment").

7Commercial Property Owner

Emergency, industrial secondary. The best-value profile in the set: real volume, Low competition, and $14 to $56 clicks nobody is fighting for. Test one crew whole building ("we clear the entire space and hand it back ready to show"), no contract ("you have a one-time problem, we are not selling you a monthly service"), and insured to your limits, leading with the COI because this buyer thinks about liability first.

Biohazard is the front door. Highest-value click available, and it opens everything else on the property.

8Residential Property Owner

Emergency, hoarding secondary. Invisible in search, so the whole profile is a copy and landing-page play on generic cleanout terms. Test you do not have to be there (a large share of this buyer is remote and it is the biggest unlock), turn speed ("empty, swept, ready to list or re-rent, usually one visit"), and price certainty before a closing.

Hoarding is the escalation. When the generic cleanout turns out to be a hoarded house, that is where a specialist beats a junk hauler.

9Commercial Insurance Adjuster

Emergency and industrial, both primary. Commercial lines only. The servable claims are biohazard and trauma scene, vandalism, debris removal inside a covered peril, and industrial cleanup after a covered spill, upset or contamination. Restoration of the building is out of scope and saying so is what makes EC easy to place alongside a restoration GC.

Angles to test: business interruption, the money accruing while the facility is down and the sharpest lever this profile has; defensible documentation, a scope and invoice that match so nobody fights a supplement; 24/7 response, catching the first-notice-of-loss moment before the assignment is made; and fits beside your GC, which positions EC as a subcontractor rather than a rival for the claim.

Not a search profile. Adjusters assign from carrier programs and TPA networks, or the restoration contractor holding the claim subs it out.

Targeting by platform

what to actually enter, per profile

Only two of these platforms take keywords. Google Search takes them directly, and YouTube and Display take them as custom segment seeds. Meta and LinkedIn do not take keywords at all. They take audience attributes, and treating them like a search engine is the most common way money disappears on those platforms.

Terms below carrying a volume figure are validated against live Keyword Planner pulls. Terms without one are proposals that have not been measured yet and should be pulled before they get budget.

New negative, discovered September 2. Seeding Keyword Planner with industrial cleaning terms returns an enormous septic tank cluster. Septic tank pumping alone is 110,000/mo, and dozens of septic variants sit at 9,900 to 74,000 each. EC does not pump septic tanks. If any industrial term runs on broad or phrase match without septic negatives, that cluster will eat the entire budget. Add septic, sewage, leach field, drain field, grease trap, fuel polishing, tank pumping as campaign-level negatives before the first industrial ad goes live. This applies to Profile 9 as well, since it carries industrial as a primary.

1The Family Caregiver

Google Search. hoarding cleaning services (5,400), hoarding cleanup (2,400), hoarder cleanout services (390), hoarding cleanup specialist (110), professional hoarder cleanup (170), house cleaning services for hoarders (590). Bid the head terms harder than the near-me variants, since the near-me set is where every local operator sits.

LSA + Business Profile. Run under House cleaning. Post before-and-after sets and the discretion protocol.

Meta. Not keywords. Target age 45 to 65, parents of aging parents, life events, and interests around caregiving, assisted living, senior care, estate planning. Lookalike seed: past hoarding customers.

LinkedIn. Not a fit. This is a personal decision, not a professional one.

YouTube / Display. Custom segment seeded with the hoarding search terms above plus URLs for IOCDF, Children of Hoarders, and senior-care sites.

Organic. how to help a hoarder (1,300 at $0.98) is the cheapest reach into this person that exists. Own it.

2The Property Manager

Google Search. Thin and low value. apartment cleanout (1,000) and eviction cleanout (390) are the only terms worth a small budget. Proposed, unmeasured: apartment turnover cleaning, turnover cleaning services, property management junk removal, multifamily junk removal.

LSA + Business Profile. Low value. This buyer does not use the map pack.

Meta. Weak. Only useful for retargeting people who hit the property-manager landing page.

LinkedIn. The real channel. Titles: Property Manager, Regional Manager, Community Manager, Maintenance Director, Director of Facilities, Portfolio Manager. Industry: Real Estate, Leasing Non-residential Real Estate. Company size 51 to 5,000.

YouTube / Display. Skip. Not where turn decisions get made.

Non-media. Credentialing platforms and the local apartment association do more here than any ad spend.

LSA + Business Profile. Junk removal category. Post the removal-record deliverable, since that is what the representative actually needs.

Meta. Life event targeting is weak for bereavement and should be handled carefully. Better used for retargeting.

LinkedIn. Titles: Probate Attorney, Estate Planning Attorney, Trust Officer, Realtor, Senior Move Manager, Estate Liquidator. Groups around probate real estate and senior move management.

YouTube / Display. Custom segment seeded with the estate cleanout terms and probate-attorney directory URLs.

Non-media. NASMM vendor membership and two estate liquidation directories beat paid reach on cost per job.

4The Plant Manager

LSA + Business Profile. No eligible category. Skip.

Meta. Not a fit.

LinkedIn. Titles: Plant Manager, Facility Manager, EHS Manager, Maintenance Manager, Turnaround Manager, Operations Director. Industries: Oil and Gas, Chemicals, Food and Beverage Manufacturing, Utilities. Time it to the two outage seasons.

YouTube / Display. Custom segment from the industrial terms, plus ISNetworld and Avetta URLs.

Non-media. Prequalification is the gate. No ad clears it.

5The Resident

Google Search. hoarding help near me (590, High), help for hoarding near me (210, High), help with hoarding cleanup (110, High). Small, contested, above $10. Send every one of these to FSSP signup, never to a quote form. Do not buy hoarding disorder treatment (5,400); that is a therapy search.

LSA + Business Profile. Yes, and the profile should carry the phased-payment language, because this person is price-checking before they call.

Meta. Strongest non-search channel for this profile. Interests around decluttering, organizing, minimalism, ADHD and executive function communities. Handle with care and never imply a diagnosis in the creative.

LinkedIn. Not a fit.

YouTube / Display. Real opportunity. Long-form hoarding content has a large audience and cheap inventory. Custom segment from the help-framed terms.

Non-media. County resource lists. Free, durable, and almost nobody maintains a national version.

6The Agency Case Manager

Google Search. No meaningful volume. Agency staff do not search for vendors, they use an approved list. Skip paid search entirely.

LSA + Business Profile. Minor. Keep the profile accurate so it corroborates a referral.

Meta. Not a fit.

LinkedIn. The only paid channel that works. Titles: Adult Protective Services Case Manager, Social Worker, Code Enforcement Officer, Housing Inspector, Conservator, Guardian, Housing Authority Manager. Industries: Government Administration, Individual and Family Services, Non-profit.

YouTube / Display. Skip.

Non-media. County vendor registration and the IOCDF task force directory are the entire channel.

8Residential Property Owner

Google Search. They never name themselves, so the terms are generic and the separation happens in the ad and on the page. house clean out (5,400), foreclosure cleanout (1,600), cleanout services (1,600), house cleanout service (1,300), property cleanout (720), trash out (720), eviction cleanout (390), whole house cleanout (170). LSA + Business Profile. Junk removal and House cleaning. Highest-volume residential profile, so the map pack matters.

Meta. Real reach. Target homeowners, real estate investors, landlord interests, and Facebook groups around rental property and REI. Lookalike seed: past cleanout customers.

LinkedIn. Thin, but Real Estate Investor and Landlord self-identifications exist and are cheap at low volume.
